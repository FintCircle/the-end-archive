# The End Archive

Scruttin

Tagline: What happened?

Core idea:
Scruttin is a searchable public archive documenting things that ended, failed, disappeared, were discontinued, cancelled, shut down, abandoned, or significantly changed.

People constantly encounter questions such as:

What happened to this app?

Why did this company shut down?

Is this product discontinued?

Why was this movie cancelled?

What happened to this website?

What replaced this service?

What's being discontinued soon?

What shut down recently?

Scruttin should become the place people go to answer those questions.

The platform combines factual records, timelines, sources, updates, and first-hand stories from people who were there.

1. Product Philosophy

Scruttin is not primarily a social network.

It is a living archive of endings and change.

Every important thing documented on Scruttin gets its own permanent Scruttin Page.

Examples:

/thing/google-domains

/thing/pocket

/thing/vine

The page should remain useful even if nobody has contributed a personal story.

Scruttin's hierarchy is:

Facts → Timeline → Explanation → Sources → First-hand stories

The platform should answer the basic question quickly before inviting the reader deeper.

3. Status System

Statuses should be extremely prominent throughout Scruttin.

Suggested statuses:

Discontinued
Product/service deliberately ended.

Shut Down
Company, service, organization or operation closed.

Cancelled
Announced/planned project stopped before completion or release.

Abandoned
Development or maintenance ceased.

Failed
Thing launched or operated but did not achieve its intended outcome.

Acquired & Ended

Merged

Replaced

Changed Direction

Shutting Down Soon

Inactive

Unknown

Do not automatically call something a "failure" simply because it ended.

Scruttin should distinguish between ending and failing.

9. Sources

Credibility is critical.

Every factual Scruttin Page should support sources.

Preferred hierarchy:

Official announcement

Company documentation

Regulatory/public records

Direct statements from people involved

Established reporting

Other credible sources

Each record should display:

Sources

Important claims should be traceable.

10. "I Was There"

This is Scruttin's distinctive community feature.

At the bottom of a Thing page:

Were you there?

Did you build it, work there, use it, invest in it, or witness what happened?

Tell your story

When submitting, users select their relationship:

I created it

I worked on it

I was involved

I used it

I witnessed it

I researched it

First-hand contributors should be visually distinguished from researched contributors.

Do NOT automatically imply employment or involvement has been verified.

Where verification exists, show:

Relationship verified

rather than a generic social-media verification badge.

11. Stories

Stories are attached to Things.

They are not independent generic blog posts.

Example:

Pocket

What shutting Pocket down looked like from inside the team

By Jane

Former employee · Relationship verified

Story body...

Stories should be typography-first and distraction-free.

Possible prompts during writing:

What was it?

What were you trying to accomplish?

When did you realize something was changing?

What happened?

Why did it end?

What did outsiders misunderstand?

What would you do differently?

These are optional prompts, not mandatory headings.

15. Contributions

Users should be able to suggest:

Suggest correction

Add source

Update this record

I was there

All community changes should enter moderation initially.

Scruttin records should not behave like unrestricted wiki pages.

Keep revision history internally.

16. Claiming / Official Participation

Organizations associated with a Thing may eventually claim their relationship.

A verified company representative could provide:

Official statement

It must be clearly labelled.

Companies must NEVER be allowed to purchase:

deletion of factual records

suppression of negative information

removal from search

favorable status

rewriting of independent reporting

Corrections should be accepted when supported by evidence.

17. Accounts

Reading does not require an account.

Accounts are needed for:

submitting Things

contributing stories

suggesting edits

saving Things

watching Things

maintaining contributor identity

Do not introduce follower counts initially.

Scruttin is about the information, not creator popularity.

18. Watch

20. Interaction

Avoid conventional engagement mechanics.

No follower race.

No public popularity numbers dominating stories.

No repost system.

No meaningless reaction bar.

A simple:

Useful

may be enough for stories.

Saving should matter more than liking.

21. Design Direction

Scruttin should feel:

Minimal

Editorial

Curious

Documentary

Trustworthy

Typography-first

Not corporate SaaS.

Not Reddit.

Not Wikipedia visually.

Not a startup-news site.

Use generous whitespace, strong headlines and restrained UI.

Status labels can provide the primary visual distinction.

Dates should be prominent.

A timeline motif can become part of Scruttin's visual identity:

2012 ─────────────────── 2026 ×

The × or endpoint visually represents the end.

26. Monetization Direction

Do not paywall the core archive.

Scruttin becomes more valuable when its pages are publicly searchable and shareable.

Potential future revenue:

Scruttin Pro
Advanced archive/search/research capabilities.

Scruttin for Teams
Private organizational postmortems and institutional memory.

Sponsorships
Clearly separated from editorial records.

Affiliate alternatives
Where a discontinued product has legitimate replacement options, with disclosure.

Research/data products
Aggregated structured information about shutdowns, discontinuations and failure patterns.

Scruttin Originals
Commissioned, professionally researched postmortems.

Do not compromise editorial records for advertisers.

28. Moderation

Because Scruttin documents failures and closures, moderation is essential.

Do not allow unsupported allegations to be presented as established fact.

Distinguish:

Verified fact

Official statement

First-hand account

Reported information

Unverified claim

Opinion

Personal stories should make clear that they represent the contributor's account.

Provide reporting and correction mechanisms.

Admin needs:

submission queue
edit queue
source review
story moderation
reports
revision history
user moderation
Thing merging
duplicate detection

29. SEO

SEO should be considered from the beginning because search questions are central to the product.

Each Thing should have useful page metadata.

Potential title pattern:

What Happened to [Thing]? — Scruttin

Description:

[Thing] was discontinued in [year]. Here's what happened, why it ended, what happened to users, and what replaced it.

Do not generate misleading titles when the reason is unknown.

Pages should be server-renderable/indexable.

Use canonical URLs.

Provide structured metadata where appropriate.

30. Core Product Rule

A visitor arriving on a Scruttin Page should be able to answer:

What was it?

What happened to it?

When did it happen?

Why did it happen, if known?

What happens now?

Where did this information come from?

within roughly the first screen or two.

Everything else adds depth.

Product Identity

Scruttin should eventually create a simple habit:

Something disappeared.

Someone asks:

"What happened to ___?"

They search Scruttin.

That's the product.
